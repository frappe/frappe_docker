Habibi Burger · PoC walkthrough · three seats

One burger, three pairs of hands, every click written down.

A WhatsApp order becomes a Sales Order, passes a payment gate, crosses a kitchen board, rides out with a courier and lands in the books — on the real instance, with the real buttons. Everything below was built and run on erp.habibi-erp.com before it was written down. The AI agent’s side of the contract lives in the AI Intake Pack; the sign-off sheet is the Shakedown Run.

AI

WhatsApp agent

middleware, not a user

Talks to the customer, prices from ERPNext, writes the draft order over REST. Hands off to a human on any complaint.

OD

Order desk

Burger Order Desk

Sales staff, escalation manager and analyst in one seat. Confirms orders, takes money, dispatches, invoices, reads the numbers.

KT

Kitchen

Burger Kitchen

One shared tablet by the pass. Sees the board, cooks, drags to Ready. Cannot raise, price or cancel anything.

CR

Courier

Burger Courier

Sees its own runs, prints the note, marks delivered. One transition, and that is the whole seat.

The three things this build proves

  • The kitchen board is native. No fork, no custom app, no restaurant module. A Kanban view over Sales Order grouped by one custom Select field. Drag-and-drop works on submitted documents because the field is marked allow-on-submit.
  • Dragging a card is not a way around the rules. Frappe’s Kanban writes the field directly — but the save still runs workflow validation. An illegal drag is refused by the server with Workflow State transition not allowed. Role gates and payment gates hold on the board exactly as they hold on the form.
  • “No cooking before payment” is enforced, not requested. The Confirmed → In Kitchen transition carries the condition advance_paid >= grand_total. Until the money is recorded the action does not exist in any menu.

ERPNext’s own status field is delivery- and billing-driven and will never reflect kitchen reality. We run our own Order Status alongside it. That is normal and expected — do not try to make the native field do this job.

Mode A — Solo

Run the whole thing as Administrator. Every action works because Administrator holds every role. Fast, and fine for a first pass — but it proves nothing about the hand-offs, and the hand-offs are the point.

Press “Solo mode” to grey out the steps that only exist to demonstrate a limit.

Mode B — Three seats

Part 0 creates three users with narrow role sets. Then hand the parts around: one person on the tablet, one on a phone as the courier, one at the desk. Forty minutes, and the client sees their own business run.

This is the mode to demo in Session 4. The refusals land better than the successes.
